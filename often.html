Eclipses occur on a somewhat regular basis, but the specific frequency depends on the type of eclipse (solar or lunar) and the specific kind of each (total, partial, or penumbral). Here's a breakdown:

Solar Eclipses:

Total Solar Eclipse: These occur roughly every 18 months somewhere on Earth. However, a total eclipse is only visible from a specific location on Earth, and the same location will experience a total eclipse roughly once every 360-410 years on average.

Partial Solar Eclipse: These occur about 2 to 5 times a year somewhere on Earth.

Annular Solar Eclipse: These occur about 2 to 4 times a year somewhere on Earth.

Lunar Eclipses:

Total Lunar Eclipse: These occur about every 2.5 years, but they are not visible from the entire Earth. The visibility depends on the location.

Partial Lunar Eclipse: These occur about 7 times a year on average.

Penumbral Lunar Eclipse: These are more common and can occur up to 7 times in a year.
